# VOICE AND WORDING

## How Wealth Code Sounds

- **Second person, close range.** "You make \$300K now. But part of you still makes financial decisions like you earn \$60K."

- **Short lines, one thought each.** Line breaks do the pacing.

- **Inner dialogue in quotes.** "I'm not qualified." "I'm too late." "Who am I to do THAT?"

- **Reframes that turn into action.** "I can't afford it" becomes "What would need to change for me to afford it?"

- **CAPS on the word that matters.** One or two per slide, never whole paragraphs.

- **Compassion before challenge.** "Thank her. She got you here. But she doesn't get to run the meeting anymore."

- **End on a move.** "Stop asking if you're behind. Start asking: WHAT'S MY NEXT MOVE?"

## The SEC Marketing Rule's Seven General Prohibitions

An advertisement may not:

- Include an untrue statement of a material fact, or leave out a fact needed to keep a statement from being misleading.

- Include a material statement of fact you could not back up if the SEC asked for proof.

- Create a misleading implication or inference about a material fact.

- Talk about potential benefits without fair and balanced treatment of the material risks or limitations.

- Reference specific investment advice in a way that is not fair and balanced.

- Include or exclude performance results, or choose time periods, in a way that is not fair and balanced.

- Otherwise be materially misleading.

*Source: SEC, Investment Adviser Marketing small entity compliance guide.*

## Ten House Rules for Every Post

1.  **Education, not advice.** Teach the concept and the questions to ask. Never tell the audience what they personally should buy, sell, or do. "Questions to ask your CPA" works. "Do this now" does not.

2.  **Every benefit gets its risk.** If a slide names an upside, a nearby slide names the tradeoff, cost, or limitation. Your debt and leverage carousel does this well: leverage can magnify gains and it can also magnify losses.

3.  **No guarantees, no promises.** Avoid "guaranteed," "risk-free," "you will," "never lose," and "always." The only exception is a contractual insurance guarantee, and it has to carry the claims-paying-ability disclosure.

4.  **No specific securities as ideas.** Teach asset classes and planning concepts. Leave out specific stock tickers, coins, funds, or price targets unless compliance has approved that exact content.

5.  **Hypotheticals are labeled and complete.** Any example with numbers says it is hypothetical, lists its assumptions, and doesn't imply real results. Change one variable at a time so the comparison stays fair.

6.  **No testimonials without compliance.** Don't post client quotes, reviews, "my client saved \$X," or reshares of client praise unless compliance has approved them with the required disclosures. Liking or replying "thank you" to a public review can count as adopting it.

7.  **Client privacy is absolute.** No client names, family details, screenshots, or stories a client could be recognized from. Use composite hypotheticals that are clearly labeled.

8.  **Tax and legal topics point to professionals.** Every tax or estate post carries the education disclosure and tells the audience to talk with a CPA or attorney about their own situation.

9.  **Archive everything.** Post only from approved accounts. Keep DMs, comments, and replies about business inside archived channels, and move detailed money questions to an approved channel.

10. **Disclose AI images when needed.** Generated or edited images should never suggest a real client or a real result. Follow any AI-disclosure requirements from the firm or the platform.

## Pre-Post Checklist

- [ ] The copy is educational and doesn't give individualized advice.

- [ ] Each benefit has a risk, cost, or limitation near it.

- [ ] No guarantee, superlative, or promissory language.

- [ ] Any numbers are sourced or labeled hypothetical, with assumptions stated.

- [ ] No specific securities, coins, or price targets unless approved.

- [ ] No client names, likenesses, or recognizable details.

- [ ] No testimonials, reviews, or reshared praise.

- [ ] The right short-form disclosure is in the caption and the full firm disclosure is reachable (profile, link in bio, or newsletter footer).

- [ ] The final art, caption, and any on-screen text went to compliance together.

- [ ] The approval date or ID is logged, and the post goes out from an archived account.

- [ ] No hashtags (house style), and the caption is within the platform's limit.

- [ ] No em dashes or tildes in any copy.
