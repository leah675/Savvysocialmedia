# Viral Verbiage and Influencer Intrigue (Compliance-Safe)

This file covers the "stop the scroll" layer: the language patterns top creators use to earn the first second, hold attention through slide 8, and get saved and shared. Every pattern here has been filtered for the Savvy brand (no fear, no shame) and for SEC/FINRA rules (no misleading implication, no promises).

> **Note on Adey / Virelish.** The user named Adey from Virelish as the model for viral verbiage. Public information on that source couldn't be found when this skill was built, so the patterns below are general high-performing creator techniques. To tune the skill to Adey's style specifically, paste real examples of her hooks, captions, or framework into `viral-voice-samples.md` in this folder; when that file has content, study it before drafting and mirror its rhythm, sentence length, and hook structures (still inside the compliance rules).

## 1. The three jobs of a viral post

1. **Stop:** the first line (or first 1.5 seconds of video) creates a pattern interrupt or a curiosity gap.
2. **Hold:** every slide or line gives a reason to see the next one. Open a loop, close it, open another.
3. **Spread:** the post is worth saving (useful later) or sending (says something the reader wants to say to someone).

Savvy content already teaches well. The viral layer is mostly about **the first line, the pacing, and the last line.**

## 2. Hook formulas

Write three per post, pick the strongest. Aim for under 12 words and under 125 characters.

| Formula | Pattern | Savvy example |
|---|---|---|
| **The contrarian truth** | Challenge something everyone believes | "Paying off all your debt might be costing you wealth." |
| **Same and different** | Two identical things, different outcome | "Same income. Wildly different tax bill." |
| **The plot twist** | Unexpected hero or angle | "Plot twist: your stay-at-home spouse could be the key to a real estate tax strategy." |
| **The identity callout** | Name exactly who this is for | "If you make six figures and still feel broke, this is for you." |
| **The overlooked question** | Something nobody thought to ask | "Before you retire, ask this about your life insurance." |
| **The myth we were taught** | Name an inherited rule | "We were taught: pay off the house, pay cash, avoid debt." |
| **The question back** | Answer a question with a sharper question | "Am I behind financially? My first question: BEHIND WHO?" |
| **The inner dialogue** | Quote the voice in her head | "'I can't afford it.' Okay. But is that actually true?" |
| **The mismatch** | Two things that should match and don't | "Your income grew. Did your identity grow with it?" |
| **The confession** | Personal, vulnerable, specific | "Motherhood didn't make me less ambitious." |
| **The reframe** | Rename the thing | "Your emergency fund isn't for emergencies. It's for exits." |
| **The gap** | Earn vs. keep, have vs. use | "The gap between what you earn and what you keep isn't a math problem." |
| **The number** | Specific, surprising, *sourced or labeled* | "Some retirees pay 0% on investment gains. Here's the window." (cite IRS thresholds) |
| **The POV** | Put them in a scene | "POV: you just got the promotion and nothing about your money changed." |
| **The "you can"** | Permission + possibility | "You can build wealth without sacrificing your life to do it." |
| **The maybe** | Soft, intimate challenge | "Maybe you don't need another strategy. Maybe you need a different room." |
| **The list tease** | Numbered, scannable | "3 questions to ask before you roll over your old 401(k)." |
| **The "nobody explains"** | The education gap (don't blame other advisors) | "Wait. Why has nobody explained this before?" |

**Hook tests:** Would she stop scrolling? Is it about *her*, not us? Does the post fully pay it off? Would compliance read it as a promise? If the hook only works because it implies a result, rewrite it.

## 3. Retention: keeping them swiping

- **One idea per slide.** If a slide needs a second sentence, the second sentence is probably the next slide.
- **End slides on a turn.** "Here's the part most people miss." "But." "Here's the catch." Each one pulls to the next swipe.
- **Open and close loops.** Slide 1 asks; slide 3 or 4 answers; slide 5 opens a new question (the tradeoff).
- **Pattern breaks.** Alternate a short punchy slide ("Knowing is a form of PROTECTION.") with a slightly fuller one.
- **Rule of three.** "Taxable now. Taxable later. Taxable never." Three beats feel complete and memorable.
- **Contrast pairs.** "Debt free vs. financially free." "Storing money vs. building wealth."
- **Reels:** hook spoken *and* on screen in the first 1 to 3 seconds; cut every 2 to 4 seconds; on-screen text carries the story with sound off; loop the ending back to the hook when you can.

## 4. Save and share triggers

People save what's useful later and share what says something for them.

- **Save:** checklists, "questions to ask your CPA," timelines, three-bucket style frameworks, document lists. Say it: "Save this for your next CPA meeting."
- **Share:** a line the reader wants someone else to hear. "Send this to the person in your life who thinks taxes just happen to them." "Send this to the friend who always says she's behind."
- **Comment:** a specific, easy question. "Which bucket do you have the least of right now?" beats "Thoughts?" Avoid prompts that invite people to post personal financial details publicly.
- **Signature lines** are repeatable and quotable: "Don't buy a bad investment for a good deduction." Build one into most posts.

## 5. Influencer intrigue techniques

What the best creators do that feels magnetic:

- **Say the quiet part kindly.** "A high income doesn't automatically make you wealthy." Honest, a little edgy, never mean.
- **Specificity over generality.** "The 401(k) form you filled out at 26 may override your will" beats "update your beneficiaries."
- **Behind the curtain.** Show how planning actually works: "When I build a plan, the first thing I look at isn't the portfolio." (No client details.)
- **Series and continuity.** Recurring names (THE SAVVY PLAYBOOK, WEALTH CODE) train the audience to look for the next one. Reference past posts: "Remember the three buckets?"
- **Vulnerability with a point.** Leah's real moments (motherhood, building a company, rebuilding) used only as she's shared them, always landing on something the reader can use.
- **Strong opinions, loosely held.** "Your investment portfolio is not your financial plan." Take a stance with something valuable on the other side.
- **Talk to one person.** "You" not "people." Write to the avatar like she's across the table.
- **Make the audience the smart one.** The ideal reaction is "Wow, I finally understand this," not "Wow, she's smart."
- **Earned curiosity.** Tease what's coming ("Friday's Playbook is about the form that overrides your will") so followers come back.

## 6. Line-level craft

- Short sentences. Fragments are fine. Line breaks do the pacing.
- Lead with verbs and nouns, cut adverbs.
- Contractions always.
- One CAPS word per slide for Wealth Code emphasis; gold or italic for the Playbook key word (specify in visual direction).
- Numbers as numerals ($500,000, 3 questions, 14 days).
- Rhythm check: read it aloud. If you'd run out of breath, break it.
- No em dashes or tildes (house style). Use a period, a colon, or a line break. These also read as more conversational and less "AI-written."
- No hashtags (house style). The hook and the topic do the discovery work.

## 7. Platform notes

| Platform | What works | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram carousel | Hook slide with big serif type, 8 slides, save/share CTA | 1080 × 1350. First 125 characters of caption show before "more." Caption max 2,200 characters. |
| Instagram/Facebook Reels, TikTok | Spoken hook + on-screen hook in first 3 seconds, captions on, 20 to 45 seconds | 1080 × 1920. Keep text out of the top and bottom 250 px. Disclosure end card at least 3 seconds. |
| Stories | Polls, quizzes, true/false | 4-frame sequence, link frame carries disclosure |
| LinkedIn | Text-first, first two lines are the hook, professional but still Savvy | Use the carousel cover as the image. Business owners, referral partners (CPAs, attorneys). |
| Facebook | Slightly longer captions, community tone | Same art as Instagram |
| Newsletter | Subject line under 50 characters, same thesis as the carousel | Full firm disclosure in the footer |

## 8. What viral tactics Savvy never uses

These work on engagement but fail the brand, the law, or both:

- Fear hooks ("You'll never retire," Halloween "scary money" hooks)
- Shame ("You're bad with money," "You're behind")
- Fake urgency ("Last chance," "Act now") unless it's a real, sourced deadline stated calmly
- "Secret," "hack," "loophole," "trick," "what the rich don't want you to know"
- Rage bait or attacks on other advisors or firms
- Income or lifestyle flexing, results screenshots, "my client made"
- Engagement bait that asks people to post personal financial details publicly
- Trend audio or memes that make the brand feel cluttered (Savvy is polished and editorial)
- Political content
- Unsourced statistics ("80% of women...")
