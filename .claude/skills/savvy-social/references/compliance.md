## 1. The rules that apply

| Rule | What it covers | What it means for posts |
|---|---|---|
| **FINRA Rule 2210**, Communications with the Public | Broker-dealer communications, including business social media | A registered principal approves static content (profiles, posts, graphics, banners, videos) before use. Real-time interactive replies can be supervised after the fact but must still follow content rules. Communications must be fair and balanced, give a sound basis for evaluating the facts, and can't contain promissory, exaggerated, or misleading claims. |
| **FINRA Regulatory Notices 10-06, 11-39, 17-18** | How 2210 applies to social media | Sharing, liking, or linking to third-party content can make it yours ("adoption"). Helping write or paying for third-party content can also make it yours ("entanglement"). |
| **SEC Marketing Rule, Rule 206(4)-1** | Advertising by registered investment advisers | Seven general prohibitions (below). Testimonials, endorsements, third-party ratings, and performance, including hypothetical performance, allowed only under strict conditions. |
| **Books and records** (SEC 204-2, FINRA 4511, SEA 17a-4) | Retention of business communications | Business posts, comments, and DMs must be captured and archived. Use only firm-approved accounts connected to archiving. |
| **FINRA Regulatory Notice 26-14** (proposal, July 2026) | Proposed update to 2210 | Would move to risk-based supervision and drop the static vs. interactive distinction. **A proposal, not a rule.** Follow current firm procedures until compliance says otherwise. |

Sources: finra.org/rules-guidance/key-topics/social-media; finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/10-06, /11-39, /17-18, /26-14; sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/small-business-compliance-guides/investment-adviser-marketing.

## 2. SEC Marketing Rule: seven general prohibitions

An advertisement may not:
1. Include an untrue statement of material fact, or omit a fact needed to keep a statement from being misleading.
2. Include a material statement of fact you couldn't substantiate if the SEC asked.
3. Create a misleading implication or inference about a material fact.
4. Discuss potential benefits without fair and balanced treatment of material risks or limitations.
5. Reference specific investment advice in a way that isn't fair and balanced.
6. Include or exclude performance results, or choose time periods, in a way that isn't fair and balanced.
7. Otherwise be materially misleading.

**Why this matters for viral copy:** a hook that promises something the post doesn't deliver, or implies a result, can be a "misleading implication" (#3). Curiosity gaps are fine. Bait-and-switch is not.

## 3. Ten house rules for every post

1. **Education, not advice.** Teach the concept and the questions to ask. "Questions to ask your CPA" works. "Do this now" does not.
2. **Every benefit gets its risk.** If a slide names an upside, a nearby slide names the tradeoff, cost, or limitation. (Model: "Leverage can magnify gains. It can also magnify losses.")
3. **No guarantees, no promises.** Avoid "guaranteed," "risk-free," "you will," "never lose," "always." Only exception: a contractual insurance guarantee, with the claims-paying-ability disclosure.
4. **No specific securities as ideas.** Teach asset classes and planning concepts. No tickers, coins, funds, carriers, or price targets unless compliance approved that exact content.
5. **Hypotheticals are labeled and complete.** Say it's hypothetical, list assumptions, don't imply real results, change one variable at a time.
6. **No testimonials without compliance.** No client quotes, reviews, "my client saved $X," or reshared praise. Liking or replying "thank you" to a public review can count as adopting it.
7. **Client privacy is absolute.** No names, family details, screenshots, or recognizable stories. Composite hypotheticals only, clearly labeled.
8. **Tax and legal topics point to professionals.** Education disclosure plus "talk with a CPA or attorney about your situation."
9. **Archive everything.** Approved accounts only. Business DMs, comments, and replies stay in archived channels; detailed money questions move to an approved channel.
10. **Disclose AI images when needed.** Generated or edited images never suggest a real client or a real result. Follow firm and platform AI-disclosure rules.

## 4. Words to swap

| Avoid | Use instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Guaranteed, risk-free, safe | Designed to, may help, can reduce | Implies a promised outcome |
| Tax-free (on its own) | Tax-free when rules are met, qualified withdrawals | Roth and HSA benefits depend on conditions |
| Loophole, secret, hack, trick | Strategy, provision, rule, planning opportunity | Suggests something improper or hidden |
| Best, top, #1, the only way | One approach, a strategy worth discussing | Unsupportable superlative |
| You will retire early / get rich | What would need to be true for… | Promissory |
| Beat the market, outperform | Align with your goals, manage risk | Performance claim |
| Free money | Employer match, tax deduction (explain it) | Misleading shorthand |
| Save $X in taxes (as fact) | In this hypothetical, the tax difference was $X | Must be labeled illustration |
| My clients saved / earned… | In a hypothetical household… | Testimonial and performance issues |
| Everyone should… | Many people may want to consider… | Suitability varies |
| Act now, last chance, before it's too late | Worth reviewing before [real date], a good time to ask | Pressure tactics; only cite real deadlines |
| You're behind / you're making a mistake | Look at what else may be possible | Fear and shame (brand rule) |
| Your advisor is failing you | Questions worth asking your advisor | Disparaging; can't substantiate |

## 5. Hooks that cross the line (and compliant rewrites)

| Risky | Why | Savvy rewrite |
|---|---|---|
| "The tax loophole the rich don't want you to know" | Loophole, secret, implied conspiracy | "The tax code isn't written against you. Most people just never get the manual." |
| "How I helped a client save $40K in taxes" | Testimonial/performance | "Same income. Wildly different tax bill. (Hypothetical, assumptions below.)" |
| "Do this before Dec 31 or lose thousands" | Fear, promissory, individualized | "Three questions worth asking your CPA before December 31." |
| "This annuity guarantees you'll never run out of money" | Guarantee, product | "Some income can be contractually guaranteed. Here's what that guarantee depends on." |
| "Retire 10 years early with this one move" | Promissory, oversimplified | "What would need to be true to retire when you want?" |
| "Miss this Medicare window and pay forever" (posted during Annual Open Enrollment) | Mixes up enrollment periods: lifetime late penalties come from missing Initial/Special Enrollment for Part B or D, not from missing the Oct 15 to Dec 7 window | "Medicare has more than one enrollment window. Knowing which one you're in matters." |

## 6. Disclosure library and when each triggers

Items 1 and 6 are language already in use. Items 2 to 5 are draft wording for compliance to review. Item 7 is from the firm website and should be confirmed with compliance for social use.

| # | Use when | Language |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Every educational post** | Savvy Financial and its affiliates do not provide tax advice. This is for educational purposes only. Please consult a qualified tax professional regarding your specific situation. |
| 2 | **Any example with numbers** | This is a hypothetical example for illustration only. It does not represent any specific client or investment, and actual results will vary. Assumptions are listed in the post. |
| 3 | **Investing, markets, allocation, diversification, cash vs. investing** | Investing involves risk, including possible loss of principal. Diversification does not guarantee a profit or protect against loss. |
| 4 | **Annuities, life insurance, LTC, disability, riders** | Guarantees are based on the claims-paying ability of the issuing insurance company. Riders may carry additional cost and have specific eligibility requirements. |
| 5 | **Digital assets, real estate funds, private investments, alternatives** | Digital assets and alternative investments can be highly volatile, illiquid, and speculative, and may not be suitable for all investors. |
| 6 | **Full firm disclosure** (newsletters, profiles, link in bio, financial plans). **Locked, never edit.** | Brokerage services provided by Maplewood Investments, Inc. Advisory services provided by Blueprint Financial Advisors LLC. Maplewood Investments, Inc. and Blueprint Financial Advisors LLC are affiliated. Maplewood Investments, Inc., does not provide tax, legal or accounting advice. Always consult a qualified tax advisory regarding your personal tax situation and a qualified legal professional for your personal estate planning situation. |
| 7 | **Website RIA disclosure** (confirm whether compliance wants it on social profiles or newsletters) | Blueprint Financial Advisors d/b/a Savvy Financial is an investment adviser registered under the Investment Advisers Act of 1940. Registration as an investment adviser does not imply any level of skill or training. |
| WC | **Wealth Code post that touches money but isn't a tax post** (used in the Wealth Code sample) | This is for educational purposes only and is not individualized financial, tax, or legal advice. |

**Stacking:** combine in this order on separate lines or as one short paragraph: 1 (or WC) → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5. Estate/legal topics: add "Please consult a qualified attorney regarding your specific situation." Reels: on-screen disclosure on the end card for at least 3 seconds, plus the full text in the caption.

**Trigger cheat sheet:**
- Tax, deductions, Roth, HSA, 529, S corp, rentals, charitable, QCD, RMD → 1
- Any $ figure, %, age-based example, comparison → 2
- Investing, market, portfolio, stock, allocation, buffer, sequence risk, cash drag → 3
- Life insurance, annuity, LTC, disability, riders, group coverage → 4
- Real estate investing, private, crypto/digital, alternatives → 5
- Wills, trusts, POA, beneficiaries, special needs → 1 + attorney line
- Pure mindset, no money concept, posted from personal account → none required (but if posted from a Savvy account or it mentions services, it's a business communication and goes through review)
- Holiday greeting → none, and no financial content or promotion in it

## 7. Social-specific situations

- **Comments asking for advice:** reply warmly, don't advise in public. "Great question, and the answer really depends on your situation. Send me a message and we can set up a time to talk it through." Then move it to an approved channel.
- **Positive client comments/reviews:** don't like, pin, reply "thank you," or reshare without compliance guidance. That can be adoption of a testimonial.
- **Resharing articles or other creators:** sharing can be adoption. Only share content compliance has approved, and don't reshare anything with performance claims.
- **Collabs, podcasts, guest posts:** if Leah helps write or pays for it, it can be entanglement. Route to compliance.
- **Paid boosts/ads:** same approval as the organic post, plus any platform "paid partnership" or financial-services ad requirements.
- **Links:** linked pages become part of the communication. Link only to approved pages (newsletter, website, official .gov sources).
- **AI or edited images:** never depict a "client" or a result. Composite people are fine if not presented as real clients.
- **Political/election content:** none.

## 8. Mindset (Wealth Code) notes

- Mindset posts are still business communications when posted from a Savvy account or when they point toward services.
- Personal stories are fine. Don't suggest a viewer will get the same results.
- No client stories, even anonymous, without compliance approval.
- When a post moves from mindset into money (investing, insurance, Social Security), add the matching disclosure.
- No statistics about women and money unless there's a current, reputable source cited in the caption or newsletter.

## 9. Pre-post checklist

- [ ] Educational, no individualized advice
- [ ] Each benefit has a risk, cost, or limitation near it
- [ ] No guarantee, superlative, or promissory language
- [ ] Numbers sourced or labeled hypothetical, with assumptions
- [ ] No specific securities, coins, carriers, or price targets
- [ ] No client names, likenesses, or recognizable details
- [ ] No testimonials, reviews, or reshared praise
- [ ] Right short-form disclosure in the caption; full firm disclosure reachable (profile, link in bio, newsletter footer)
- [ ] Final art, caption, and on-screen text submitted to compliance together
- [ ] Approval date/ID logged; posted from an archived account
- [ ] No hashtags; caption within platform limit
- [ ] No em dashes or tildes
