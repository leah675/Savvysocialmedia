# StoryBrand for Savvy Posts

Donald Miller's StoryBrand framework (SB7) says people don't buy from the brand with the best product; they buy from the brand that tells the clearest story about *them*. The customer is the hero. The brand is the guide. That fits Savvy perfectly: "Be the director, not the extra." Leah doesn't star in the movie. She hands the audience the script.

This file adapts the seven elements to social posts and to compliance, because the classic SB7 "failure" stakes can drift into fear-selling, which both the brand and the SEC Marketing Rule push against.

## The seven elements, Savvy edition

| SB7 element | What it means | Savvy translation | Example (Playbook: group coverage ends at retirement) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. A character** | The hero wants something specific | The avatar and the *life* they want, not the account balance | A couple three years from retirement who want to travel and know the survivor is taken care of |
| **2. Has a problem** | External, internal, philosophical | **External:** the planning gap. **Internal:** how it feels. **Philosophical:** why it shouldn't be this way | External: life insurance ends with the job. Internal: "We thought we were covered." Philosophical: you shouldn't find out what you have at the worst possible moment |
| **3. And meets a guide** | Empathy + authority | Empathy: "This is common, and it's not a character flaw." Authority: a real rule explained simply. **Never brag; let the clarity be the credential** | "Group term coverage usually ends when employment ends. Some plans let you convert it." |
| **4. Who gives them a plan** | Simple steps | 2 to 4 steps or questions to ask. The "Questions to ask your advisor or CPA" slide *is* the plan | What ends? Can it be converted? What would the survivor need? What would it cost? |
| **5. And calls them to action** | Direct and transitional CTAs | **Direct:** "Questions about your situation? Send me a message." **Transitional:** save this, send it to someone, comment, newsletter link | "Save this for your next review." |
| **6. That helps them avoid failure** | The stakes | **Reframe as missed opportunity, not fear.** Name what's at stake calmly and factually; never "you'll never retire" | "A retirement plan that covers income but not the survivor has a gap worth closing." |
| **7. And ends in a success** | The transformation | Connect to LifeWealth outcomes: freedom, choice, time, family, security, legacy. "I understand more. I see more possibilities. I know what I can do next." | "Retirement plans cover income. Make sure yours covers the survivor too." |

## The one-line BrandScript

Use this as a gut check for any post:

> **[Avatar] wants [life outcome]. But [external problem] makes them feel [internal problem], and that's not right because [philosophical]. Savvy understands [empathy] and knows [authority]. Here's the plan: [steps]. [CTA] so you can [success] instead of [missed opportunity].**

Example (Wealth Code: "Do you know where it all is?"):
> A woman rebuilding her life wants to feel in control of her future. But the accounts were always "his job," which makes her feel exposed, and nobody should learn what they have at the worst moment. Savvy has seen this many times and knows exactly what to list. The plan: every account, where it's held, the beneficiary, who to call. Start the list this week, so you feel protected instead of guessing.

## Mapping SB7 onto the formats

**Playbook 8-slide carousel**
| Slide | SB7 |
|---|---|
| 1 Hook | Character + problem in one line |
| 2 The belief/situation | External problem, often the "myth we were taught" |
| 3 The idea | Guide (authority, plain language) |
| 4 Labeled hypothetical | Guide shows the math |
| 5 What it isn't | Guide's honesty (builds trust) |
| 6 Tradeoffs | Stakes, fair and balanced |
| 7 Questions to ask | Plan |
| 8 Takeaway + sign-off | Success + CTA |

**Wealth Code carousel**
| Slide | SB7 |
|---|---|
| 1 Hook that names her | Character |
| 2 Scene / inner dialogue | Internal problem |
| 3 Compassion | Guide (empathy) |
| 4 The turn | Philosophical problem: "she doesn't get to run the meeting anymore" |
| 5 Reframe / better questions | Plan begins |
| 6 Money bridge | Plan (one concrete money move) |
| 7 Permission line | Success |
| 8 Takeaway + sign-off | CTA (reflective question in caption) |

**Caption:** hook (character + problem) → short story (internal) → the idea (guide) → one move (plan) → question or save/share (CTA) → disclosure.

**Reel:** 0 to 3 sec hook (character + problem) → situation and idea (guide) → one rule + tradeoff (stakes) → what to ask (plan) → end card (success + CTA + disclosure).

## Guardrails

- The guide shows empathy *and* authority. Authority comes from clear explanation and real rules, not from credentials stacked in the copy, client wins, or results. (Leah's designations belong in the profile and newsletter signature.)
- Stakes are opportunity-framed. The brand rule: "Savvy creates urgency through opportunity, not shame." The compliance rule: no fear-based or promissory claims.
- Success is a life outcome, never a promised financial result. "Freedom to choose" is fine. "Retire at 55" is not.
- Don't overuse the direct CTA. Most posts get a transitional CTA (save, share, comment). Use "send me a message" when the topic naturally needs personal planning.
