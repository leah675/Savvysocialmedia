Your Initial Enrollment Period is about 7 months around your 65th birthday: 3 months before, your birthday month, and 3 months after.

SLIDE 4: Why it matters: if you don't sign up for Part B or Part D when you're first eligible, and you don't have other qualifying coverage, a late enrollment penalty can be added to your premium. In many cases, for as long as you have that coverage.

SLIDE 5: Still working at 65? Coverage through a current employer can let you delay, with a Special Enrollment Period after the job or the coverage ends.
What usually doesn't count as current employer coverage: COBRA and retiree coverage.

SLIDE 6: One more timing detail: if you have an HSA, contributions have to stop before Medicare coverage begins, and Part A can start retroactively. Talk to your CPA about the timing.

SLIDE 7: Ask before you retire:
When does my Initial Enrollment Period start?
Does my current coverage count?
Do I have an HSA that needs a stop date?
What happens to my spouse's coverage?

## Caption
Medicare has more than one enrollment window. Mixing them up can be expensive.

Open Enrollment started this week, and it's the one everyone talks about. From October 15 to December 7, people already on Medicare can review and switch their coverage for January 1.

But the window that can follow you for years is the first one. Your Initial Enrollment Period runs about 7 months around your 65th birthday. Miss it for Part B or Part D without other qualifying coverage, and a late enrollment penalty can be added to your premium, often for as long as you have that coverage.

If you're still working at 65, coverage through a current employer may let you delay. COBRA and retiree coverage usually don't count. And if you have an HSA, contributions need to stop before Medicare begins, which is easy to miss.

None of this is complicated once you see the calendar. It just has to be on your plan before your last day of work, not after.

## Facts to verify before submitting
- Annual Open Enrollment: Oct 15 to Dec 7, 2026, changes effective Jan 1, 2027 (medicare.gov/health-drug-plans/open-enrollment)
- Initial Enrollment Period: 7 months (3 before, birthday month, 3 after) (medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/sign-up/when-does-medicare-coverage-start)
- Part B late penalty: 10% for each full 12-month period without Part B when eligible, generally for as long as you have Part B (medicare.gov/basics/costs/medicare-costs/avoid-penalties)
- Part D late penalty: based on months without Part D or other creditable drug coverage, generally for as long as you have Part D (same source)
- Special Enrollment Period: 8 months after employment or employer group coverage ends; COBRA and retiree coverage don't count as current employment coverage (medicare.gov)
- HSA: no contributions once enrolled in Medicare; Part A can be retroactive up to 6 months when enrolling after 65 (IRS Pub. 969 and Medicare.gov)
