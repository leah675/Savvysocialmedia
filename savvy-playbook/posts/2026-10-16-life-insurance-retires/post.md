## Carousel copy (8 slides)
SLIDE 1: Your life insurance might retire the same day you do.
SLIDE 2: Many people's only life insurance comes through work. Group term coverage usually ends when employment ends.
SLIDE 3: That can include coverage on a spouse through the same employer plan.
SLIDE 4: Why it matters: if one spouse passes, a pension, annuity income, or one Social Security check may stop. The survivor's expenses don't drop by half.
SLIDE 5: Some group plans let you convert or carry coverage after you leave. The cost and terms vary, so compare them before your last day.
SLIDE 6: Options to price: converting group coverage, a new individual term or permanent policy, or using other assets to cover the gap. Each has costs, underwriting, and tradeoffs.
SLIDE 7: Ask before you retire: What coverage ends? Can it be converted? What would the survivor need? What would it cost to replace?
SLIDE 8: Retirement plans cover income. Make sure yours covers the survivor too. THE SAVVY PLAYBOOK. Don't just make more. Make what you have work smarter.

## Caption
Your life insurance might retire the same day you do.

For many couples, the only coverage they have comes through work, sometimes on both spouses. When the job ends, that coverage usually ends with it. And if one spouse passes in retirement, part of the household income can stop while most of the bills keep coming.

The fix starts with a simple review before your last day: what ends, what can be converted, and what the surviving spouse would actually need. Then compare the cost of keeping, replacing, or self-funding that protection.

Have you checked what happens to your coverage when you retire? Save this for your next review.
