**Savvy Financial — Brand Voice Guide**

**The Brand in One Sentence**

**Savvy Financial makes sophisticated financial planning feel clear, personal, elevating, and connected to the life you actually want to live.**

We aren't here just to help people accumulate more.

We're here to help them **use wealth intentionally—to create freedom, protect what matters, build a meaningful life today, and create a legacy that lasts.**

**1. The Savvy Voice**

**Confident, not corporate.**

We know our stuff. We don't need complicated language to prove it.

Translate complexity into language people actually understand.

**Instead of:**  
*"We utilize comprehensive tax-mitigation and asset-allocation strategies."*

**Savvy:**  
*"Making more money is great. Keeping more of it matters too."*

**Smart, but conversational.**

Savvy should sound like the **really smart friend you trust with the biggest financial decisions of your life**—not a textbook, Wall Street commercial, or financial institution.

Use:

- Contractions

- Short sentences

- Questions

- Real-life examples

- Analogies

- Occasional humor

- Plain English

Technical terminology is appropriate when necessary, but **explain it immediately.**

**Direct, with a little edge.**

We are willing to challenge conventional financial advice.

Examples:

**"Your investment portfolio is not your financial plan."**

**"A high income doesn't automatically make you wealthy."**

**"The goal isn't to retire with the biggest number possible. The goal is to build a life you don't need to escape from."**

We don't create controversy simply for engagement. We challenge ideas when there is something valuable on the other side.

**Elevating, never diminishing.**

Savvy should make people feel like **more is possible**.

We don't use shame, fear, or financial jargon to make someone feel like they aren't doing enough.

We elevate the conversation.

We help people see opportunities they didn't know existed, ask better questions, make more intentional decisions, and raise their expectations for what financial planning can actually do for their lives.

The feeling should be:

**"I understand more. I see more possibilities. I know what I can do next."**

**2. The Emotional Core**

Savvy isn't ultimately about money.

It's about what money makes possible.

**Freedom.  
Choice.  
Time.  
Family.  
Experiences.  
Security.  
Impact.  
Legacy.**

Financial strategies are the mechanism.

**LifeWealth is the outcome.**

Our content should regularly connect the financial decision back to the human reason behind it.

Don't just explain *what* someone should consider.

Explain **why it matters to their life.**

Every interaction should elevate the client's understanding of what's possible with the wealth they've created.

**3. The Savvy Woman**

A significant part of the Savvy voice speaks directly to ambitious women.

She may be:

A mom.  
A founder.  
A high-income professional.  
A wife.  
A caregiver.  
A woman rebuilding after divorce or loss.  
Or several of those at once.

She doesn't need someone telling her to stop buying coffee.

She needs sophisticated advice that recognizes the complexity of her life.

Our message to her is:

**You can build wealth without sacrificing your life to do it.**

**You can be ambitious and present.**

**You can want more without being ungrateful for what you already have.**

**You can create financial independence while building a beautiful life right now.**

Savvy doesn't tell women what they *should* want.

**We elevate their understanding of their options so they can decide what they want their wealth to make possible.**

**4. Expertise Without Intimidation**

Savvy works with complex strategies, but the communication should rarely *feel* complex.

We may discuss:

Tax strategy.  
Estate planning.  
Investment management.  
Business planning.  
Insurance.  
Retirement.  
Charitable planning.  
Advanced wealth strategies.

But the audience shouldn't finish reading and think:

*"Wow, she's smart."*

The better reaction is:

**"Wow. I finally understand this."**

Clarity demonstrates expertise better than jargon.

Education should feel **elevating rather than overwhelming**. The goal isn't to show people how complicated finance can be. It's to make sophisticated planning accessible enough that they can participate confidently in the conversation.

**5. Life First. Money Second.**

Traditional financial planning often begins with:

*"How much do you need to retire?"*

Savvy asks:

**"What do you actually want your life to look like?"**

Then we determine how money can help make that possible.

That philosophy should show up consistently across the brand.

We aren't building financial plans in isolation.

We're helping people coordinate:

**Money + Taxes + Business + Family + Protection + Lifestyle + Legacy**

because every financial decision affects something else.

**The goal is to elevate financial planning from managing accounts to intentionally designing what wealth can do for a life.**

**6. Content Voice**

Social content should feel different from a financial-services advertisement.

**Lead with the person, not the product.**

Don't open with:

*"Here are three tax strategies business owners should consider."*

Try:

**"You can make \$500,000 a year and still be terrible at building wealth."**

Then teach.

**Use the pattern:**

**HOOK → STORY/PROBLEM → REFRAME → VALUE → TAKEAWAY**

Whenever possible, make someone **feel something before asking them to learn something.**

Our content shouldn't just educate.

**It should elevate the conversation people are having about money.**

**7. The Three Main Content Energies**

**EDUCATE**

*"Wait…why has nobody explained this to me before?"*

Make complex financial concepts simple and useful.

Topics include tax strategy, investing, estate planning, business planning, and wealth-building.

**CHALLENGE**

*"I've never thought about money that way."*

Question outdated beliefs around money, success, retirement, motherhood, and wealth.

Examples:

**"Being good with money doesn't mean being afraid to spend it."**

**"Wealth isn't how much money you refuse to spend."**

**CONNECT**

*"She gets it."*

This is where Leah's personal voice becomes especially important.

Motherhood.  
Building a company.  
Ambition.  
Grief.  
Growth.  
Marriage.  
Travel.  
Healing.  
Becoming.

Not every personal story needs a financial lesson.

Sometimes **connection is the strategy.**

Together, these three content energies should do something bigger:

**Elevate what people expect from financial advice—and what they believe is possible for their own lives.**

**9. Language to Avoid**

Avoid sounding like traditional financial-services marketing.

Minimize phrases such as:

*"Secure your financial future."*

*"Achieve your financial goals."*

*"Your trusted financial partner."*

*"Customized solutions tailored to your unique needs."*

*"Navigate today's complex financial landscape."*

They're technically fine.

They're also forgettable.

Avoid fear-based messaging whenever possible:

**"You're behind."**  
**"You're making a huge mistake."**  
**"You'll never retire."**

Savvy creates urgency through **opportunity**, not shame.

The brand should elevate people rather than make them feel inadequate.

We don't say:

**"Look at everything you're doing wrong."**

We say:

**"Look at what else may be possible."**

**Savvy's voice should be:**

Warm.

Inclusive.  
Clear.  
Elevating.  
Trustworthy.

**Leah is the personality.  
Savvy is the authority.**

Together, they create a brand that feels both **human and elevated**.

**11. Our Ideal Reader Reaction**

Every piece of Savvy content should ideally create at least one of these reactions:

**"I didn't know that."**

**"I need to think differently about this."**

**"That's exactly how I feel."**

**"Why hasn't my advisor talked to me about this?"**

**"I didn't realize that was an option."**

**"I want this kind of planning."**

And perhaps most importantly:

**"I trust her."**

The audience should leave feeling like the conversation **elevated their thinking**, even if they never become a client.

**12. The Savvy Filter**

Before publishing anything, ask:

**Does this sound like a human or a financial institution?**

**Could someone understand it without a finance degree?**

**Does it teach, challenge, connect—or ideally do more than one?**

**Does it elevate the conversation rather than rely on fear or shame?**

**Does it help someone see greater possibilities for their wealth or their life?**

**Does it connect money to something bigger than money?**

**Would someone recognize this as Savvy if the logo disappeared?**

If the answer to the last question is yes, the brand voice is working.

**Savvy in Five Words**

**Smart. Human. Bold. Elevating. Intentional.**

**What "Elevating" Means to Savvy**

We help people **think bigger about what their wealth can do.**

Our conversations, strategies, and content should leave people with greater clarity, better options, and a higher standard for what financial planning can look like.

We don't elevate by making wealth feel exclusive or complicated.

**We elevate by making sophisticated ideas understandable, possibilities visible, and intentional action achievable.**

**The Ultimate Brand Feeling**

**Build wealth. Live fully. Make it mean something.**
